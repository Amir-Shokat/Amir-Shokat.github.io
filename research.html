Research

MSc thesis · Utrecht University · 2024

Warming Trends: Analysing the Impact of Temperature Anomaly on the Risk and Return of the US Stock Market

Do unusually hot or cold months at a company's headquarters show up in its stock returns or volatility? I built a panel of about 18,000 US firms from 1995 to 2024, matched each firm to local temperature data, and estimated fixed-effects regressions.

Later I rebuilt the whole pipeline from scratch and audited it. The original matched weather to firms by city name only, so 45% of firm-months got another town's weather (there are a lot of Springfields). With that fixed, there is no detectable temperature effect. That's a less exciting headline, but it's the right one.
