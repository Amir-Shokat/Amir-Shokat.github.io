The data problem

Risk reports are only as good as their data. In Iran, market data is spread over several public websites with different formats, Persian text in mixed character sets, and dates on the Persian calendar. At the bank, collecting it by hand took hours for every report.

I wrote tools that collect it automatically: daily prices and trading volumes from the Tehran Stock Exchange, the major shareholders of each fund, and a daily snapshot of every mutual fund from the national fund registry (FIPIRAN).

Who stands behind the funds?

Every Iranian mutual fund has a manager that invests the money and a guarantor, usually a bank or broker, that promises to buy back units when investors want to cash out. Not every fund has one: exchange-traded funds, for example, can be sold on the stock exchange instead. If a guarantor gets into trouble, every fund it backs is affected at once.

Fund management is fragmented, with more than 150 managers. Only a minority of funds have a liquidity guarantor at all, but for those that do, the guarantees sit with very few institutions. That risk is invisible when you look at funds one by one, and obvious when you map the whole market.

The tools

  • Exchange data: full price histories, the "base volume" that decides how far a price may move each day, and daily major-shareholder lists, with automatic retries when the exchange's servers are busy.
  • Fund panel: a day-by-day table of every fund (NAV, returns, asset mix, size, manager and guarantor) that can be built from scratch or updated with just the new days.
  • Name cleaning: the same firm appears under several spellings (Arabic or Persian letters, legal suffixes, extra spaces). Without cleaning, six managers were counted twice.

What I learned

  • Look at the whole system, not single parts. The most important risk here only appears in the network view.
  • Clean names before you count. Simple text normalisation changed the concentration numbers.
  • Be a good API citizen. Rate limits, retries and skipping weekends (Thursday and Friday in Iran) cut the number of requests by about 30%.
