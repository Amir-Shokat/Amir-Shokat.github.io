The market

Iran has around 500 mutual funds. Each reports its size, unit price and holdings to the national fund registry (FIPIRAN) every day. Adding them up gives the size of the whole market, split by the kind of fund.

Money in and out

Assets grow for two reasons: the funds' investments gain value, and investors put in new money. The second part, the net flow, shows what investors actually choose. I measure it from the change in the number of units each fund has outstanding, valued at that day's redemption price.

Are ETFs fairly priced?

Exchange-traded funds trade on the stock exchange at a market price, which can drift away from the value of what the fund holds (its NAV). A price above NAV is a premium, below it a discount. Large gaps mean buyers are overpaying, or that the market maker is not doing its job.

From Power BI to the web

This started as a 17-page Power BI report that I refreshed by hand from Excel exports. Rebuilding it turned up real errors, which are fixed here:

  • The flow pages were empty. They used a column that no longer existed in the data. Flows are now computed from units outstanding, which works for every fund, ETFs included.
  • "Market share by fund type" showed funds, not types. The pie chart was wired to the fund name.
  • ETF premiums used dividend-adjusted prices, which shift the past price history every time a fund pays out. Premiums now use the real traded price.
  • Gold counted as stocks. Before the registry added a commodity field, gold funds reported their gold certificates as "stock", which overstated how much of the market was in equities.
  • Days with missing funds looked like crashes. The registry sometimes returns only a fraction of the funds; those days are dropped.
  • Mixed units. Two holdings columns were in percent while the rest were fractions.
  • Reported returns did not match prices. The registry's daily-return field often disagrees with the fund's own unit price (one day showed +335% while the price fell 5%), which made some funds look like they had gained 2,000% in a year. Returns now come from the unit price, with payouts added back.
  • One fixed 30% safe rate for every Sharpe ratio. For bond funds, whose value barely moves, that produces huge meaningless numbers. Ratios are now shown only for funds that take market risk, against the median return of fixed-income funds.

The data started fresh in November 2026 and grows by a week at each update, so longer returns and the risk measures (which need about three months of daily data) fill in over time. One command collects the new days with tehran-market-data, rebuilds these files and publishes the site.
