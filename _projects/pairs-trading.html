The idea in plain words

Think of two coffee chains in the same city. Their share prices react to the same news (coffee prices, consumer spending, interest rates), so they tend to move together. Sometimes one runs ahead for a few days for no lasting reason. A pairs trader sells the one that ran ahead and buys the one that lagged, and closes the trade when the gap closes.

Because the trader is long one stock and short the other, a market-wide rise or fall mostly cancels out. The bet is only on the gap.

Step 1: are the two stocks really tied together?

Two prices can look related on a chart by pure chance. The statistical test used here, cointegration, asks a sharper question: does the gap between them keep pulling back to its normal level? If it does, the gap is tradable. If it wanders off and never comes back, it isn't.

In the course, every pair of stocks on the exchange was tested. Only two passed at the strict 1% level (NVIDIA with Cisco, and ING with Santander), and those were the only pairs the bot traded. On real daily data, NVIDIA and Cisco are not tied together (p = 0.87), so the code warns you before trading a pair that fails the test.

The risk: relationships break

Tick Break the relationship halfway in the simulator. From day 300 the gap no longer pulls back, as can happen after a merger, a scandal or a change in one company's business. The strategy keeps betting on a return that never comes, and losses pile up.

That's why the real bot had strict risk limits: a maximum position of 100 shares per stock, and an automatic close-out if the two legs of a trade drifted more than 20% away from the intended hedge ratio.

What I learned

  • Test before you trust a pattern. Many pairs "look" related but fail a proper statistical test.
  • Costs decide everything. Push the trading cost slider up: a strategy that trades too often can turn a good signal into a loss.
  • Execution details matter. In the original version the second leg of each trade was sized on the order, not on what actually got filled, which could leave a position unhedged. The rebuild sizes it on real fills.
